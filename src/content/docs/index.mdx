## Why this exists

"Vibe coding" — describing what you want in plain language and letting an AI agent write the code — is easiest to learn by doing, but easy to do badly. Skip straight to "build me an app" and you get something that looks plausible and falls apart the moment a friend actually uses it. This playbook is the middle path: enough structure to think before you build, light enough that it doesn't feel like a term project.

Each step is a small, versioned document. You fill it in — by hand, or by handing the matching **LLM prompt** to your AI tool of choice along with your previous step's document. The output of one step becomes the input to the next, so the whole thing chains from a rough problem statement to a published app.

## The rule of thumb

Not every step needs to be deep. A weekend project might spend five minutes on `07-testing` and two hours on `05-prototype`. The point isn't to fill in every heading — it's to not skip a step entirely just because it's less fun than writing code. Read [How to use this playbook](how-to-use/) before you start.
